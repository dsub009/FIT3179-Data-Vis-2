2 · The shelf

What's on the shelf?

Snacks and sweets are the clear outlier. Half of them have more than 39.7 g of sugar per 100 g. Pasta, rice and grains have a median of just 0.6 g.

Star ratings follow the same pattern. 11 of the 18 snacks and sweets earn only half a star. All 20 pasta, rice and grain products earn 3 stars or more.

Source: OpenHSR (N'kam Suguem and Lafargue, 2025). 246 Australian packaged products.

Sweeter products carry more energy, and the sweetest earn the lowest ratings. Drag a box on the chart to see which food types sit in each area.

Sugar is easier to picture in teaspoons. A typical 100 g of snacks and sweets holds about 10. Pasta, rice and grains hold less than a quarter of one.

Source: OpenHSR (N'kam Suguem and Lafargue, 2025). Sugars include natural sugars, such as those in milk and fruit.

Sugar is not the only difference. Snacks lead on sugar and saturated fat. Vegetables and canned foods lead on salt. Meat leads on protein, and breakfast cereals lead on fibre.

Source: OpenHSR (N'kam Suguem and Lafargue, 2025). Fibre shows 0.0 where the label gives none.

Now compare stores. Aldi products average 3.5 stars in this sample. Woolworths averages 2.6. It looks like a clear win for Aldi.

But the gap is partly about what each store sells here. Aldi has many frozen, pasta and rice products. Woolworths has more snacks and drinks.

Source: OpenHSR (N'kam Suguem and Lafargue, 2025). Products sold at both stores count for each store. Coles products appear only alongside Woolworths in this dataset, so Coles is not compared.

Compare like with like and the story changes. Aldi leads in bakery and sauces. Woolworths leads in vegetables and canned food.

4 · The supply

Where does Australia's food come from?

New Zealand is the biggest source, at A$3.1 billion. The United States and China follow.

Five Southeast Asian countries send A$5.7 billion between them. Malaysia ranks eighth, at A$851 million.

5 · The takeaway

What to remember at the shelf
